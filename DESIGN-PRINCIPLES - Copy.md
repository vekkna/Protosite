### Board Game Design Principles (Tailored for *Council of Enemies*)
A working reference for critiquing and iterating on *Council of Enemies*. Adapted and condensed from Daniel Piechnick's game-design articles (https://daniel.games/, designer of *Radlands*).

These principles are strong defaults and a critique checklist, not laws. Whenever a default here conflicts with the "Notes for *Council of Enemies*" section at the end, follow the project overrides—while still flagging the complexity or feelbad cost of breaking the default.

---

#### 0. Quick review checklist
Run any proposed mechanic, card, or rule change through these questions:
1. **Core:** Does it serve the game's central activity (deduction and choice manipulation), or is it a side system?
2. **Depth:Complexity:** Does it add more gameplay than the rules, text, and mental load it costs? Could a version half as complex keep 90% of the value?
3. **Rules vs. components:** Could a component change (card mix, board track, pile count) fix this instead of adding a rule?
4. **Turn:** Does it add a step, phase, maintenance task, or low-stakes choice to every turn?
5. **Choices:** Does it add a "can't" restriction where an incentive or component structure would do? Does it accidentally allow a player to force an outcome?
6. **Evaluable:** Can players judge this choice from public information and deduction, or is it a lucky dip?
7. **Context:** Is the option sometimes good and sometimes bad depending on the table state, or is it always/never taken?
8. **Feel:** Does it create unfun friction (arbitrary kingmaking, being locked out of acting, visibly hopeless positions) rather than productive tension?
