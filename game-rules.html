Seize the Day

Rules supplied for this game. The app uses the outlined circular terrain footprint as the feature. Some illustrated examples in this rules draft are still marked TODO.

Overview
Seize the Day is a game of battles for two players. Each player commands an army, moving units across a tabletop battlefield and using dice to resolve shooting and close combat.
You do not need to collect miniatures or prepare an army beforehand. The units are represented by cards, which act as both playing pieces and reference cards. At the start of the game, you take turns choosing these units and placing terrain, creating your armies and battlefield together.
During the game, you move and turn your units to outmanoeuvre the enemy. Good positioning and timing matters: you can gain an advantage by attacking from the flank, holding useful terrain, or staying outside an enemy’s reach.
The game is played over a series of rounds. At the start of each round, both players secretly bid for control of the turn order. A lower bid gives you more control over who acts when, but a higher bid lets you command more units. You must decide which matters more each round.
You win when half your opponent’s units have routed. The rules are deliberately simple and abstract, focusing on the key decisions of a tabletop battle rather than detailed simulation.

Unit basics
Fronts and flanks
A unit’s front is its front edge, including both front corners. Everything else (both side edges, both rear corners and the rear edge) is its flank.
If a unit touches any part of an enemy’s flank, it counts as touching that enemy’s flank, even if it also touches the enemy’s front.


Figure 1. A unit's front and flank
Stats
Pace: how far the unit can move forwards, in inches.
Mobility: how many pivots the unit can make in each activation, as well as its action. Units with Mobility 3 or more are hard to shoot (see Evasion).
Melee: how many dice the unit starts with when it strikes.
Shoot: how many dice the unit starts with when it shoots. A unit with no Shoot value can’t shoot.
Range: how far the unit can shoot, in inches.
Defence: the score each attack die needs to give the unit strain. Units with Defence 4 or less are not slowed by slowing terrain.
An asterisk (*) after a Melee or Shoot value means that attack is armour-piercing.

Setup
Prepare the battlefield
Play on an area 4 feet wide and 3 feet deep, with the players sitting opposite each other at the long edges.
Each player’s deployment zone is the strip along their edge, 6 inches deep. This leaves 24 inches between the zones. On a different-sized table, make both zones the same depth and keep the gap between them as close to 24 inches as you can.

First game
For your first game, you can use the prepared armies and battlefield below instead of drafting. Choose a random player to count as having picked first in the draft. This decides who wins a tied bid in round 1. Otherwise, continue with Prepare the Draft Pool.
Prepare the draft pool
Randomly decide which player uses the dark side of the unit cards. The other player uses the light side. The two sides differ only in appearance.
Choose 12 unit cards at random. Lay them out in an evenly spaced grid between the deployment zones, 4 cards across and 3 deep. No card may touch a deployment zone.
Shuffle the terrain tiles so that it’s random which side of each tile is up, then stack them. Players may look at the top tile but not the ones beneath it.
Randomly choose who picks first.
If you have miniatures, you can choose the 12 units to suit your collection instead of drawing them at random, and place the miniatures on the cards.

Draft units and place terrain
Players take turns to pick, starting with the first player, until all 12 units are deployed. On your pick:
Choose a unit from the draft pool. Leave it where it is for now.
Take the top terrain tile without flipping it over.
Place or discard the tile. If it shows only open ground, or you can’t place it legally, return it to the box. Otherwise, place it so that it:
overlaps the unit you chose, and
doesn’t touch another terrain tile, a deployment zone or the battlefield’s edge.
You may rotate it however you like. If it covers other undrafted units, put them back on top of the tile in the same positions.
Deploy the unit. Place the unit you chose entirely inside your deployment zone, facing any direction, without overlapping another unit.

Winning the game
You win immediately when at least half your opponent’s starting units, rounded up, have routed. For example, with 6 units each, you win when the third enemy unit routs. 
Round sequence
Play rounds until someone wins. Each round has three steps:
Bid for Master Tactician
Command units
Clean up
Bid for Master Tactician
The Master Tactician controls the timing of the round, but usually commands fewer units.
Each player secretly chooses a bid, from 1 up to the number of units they have on the battlefield. Set a die to your bid and keep it covered with your hand.
Reveal both bids at the same time.
Each player takes as many command markers as they bid.
The player with the lower bid is the Master Tactician for this round. If the bids are equal, last round’s Master Tactician keeps the title. In round 1, the player who picked second in the draft wins a tie.
Example: Brian secretly sets a die to 4, Irina secretly sets one to 2.  They reveal them at the same time. Brian gets 4 command markers, Irina 2. Irina becomes the Master Tactician.
Command units
The command phase has up to three turns:
The Master Tactician’s first turn. The Master Tactician activates units one at a time. After each activation, they choose whether to activate another unit or pass. They may pass without activating any units.
The other player’s turn. The other player activates units in the same way until they pass. This is their only turn this round.
The Master Tactician’s second turn. The Master Tactician may spend any command markers they have left in the same way, then passes.
A player with no command markers left must pass. Any command markers not spent by the end of the phase are lost.

Measuring, stating intentions and resolving disputes
You may measure distances and check lines of sight at any time. When you move a unit, state your intended result so that disagreements can be resolved before the position becomes difficult to reconstruct. For example:
I will move my archers here, where they’ll have a clear shot at your spearmen and remain outside your cavalry's charge range.
I will pivot my swords to protect their flank from your cavalry.
I will leave my fanatics 6 inches from your Heavy Infantry, so I can charge next round but you can’t charge me.
If you can’t resolve a dispute, roll a die to determine who’s right.
You can use your overview cards to work out hypothetical moves before making them.
Activating Units
Choose one of your units that doesn’t have a command marker on it.
Place one of your command markers on it.
The unit may take one action: Move, Shoot, Strike, Extra Pivot or Rally. It may also pivot (see Pivoting).
Because the marker stays on the unit until clean-up, each unit can be activated only once per round.
Pivoting
A pivot turns a unit up to 90 degrees around its centre. In each activation, a unit may make as many pivots as its Mobility, as well as taking its action. It can pivot before its action, after it, or partway through a Move (but see Charging).
A unit can’t pivot:
while it is touching an enemy, or
if its card would touch another unit, impassable terrain or the battlefield’s edge at any point while turning.
These rules also apply to the extra pivot from the Extra Pivot action.

Actions
Move
Choose a direction, then move the unit in a straight line, either forwards up to its Pace or backwards up to half its Pace. It can’t switch between forwards and backwards during the Move.
Units can’t overlap other units, impassable terrain, or the battlefield’s edge.
If the unit pivots partway through, it carries on in the same direction relative to its new facing. Pivots don’t use up distance, but the total distance moved can’t go over the limit.
A unit touching an enemy can’t Move, unless it belongs to the Master Tactician. Even then, it can’t pivot until it is no longer touching the enemy.

Charging
A unit can only move into contact with an enemy by charging: moving forwards without pivoting during the Move. It may pivot before the Move. Any other Move must stop before touching an enemy.
When a charging unit touches one or more enemies:
it stops moving,
it can’t pivot for the rest of this activation, and
it may immediately Strike one enemy it is touching, as a free extra action.

Shoot
A unit can Shoot only if it has a Shoot value and isn’t touching an enemy. Choose a target that meets all of these conditions:
Not in contact: it isn’t touching any of your units.
In range: a straight line from the centre of the shooter’s front edge to the nearest point of the target is no longer than the shooter’s Range.
In line of sight: the whole target card can be seen from the centre of the shooter’s front edge. No straight line from that point to any part of the target may pass through the shooter itself, another unit, concealing terrain or elevated terrain (see Elevated) before reaching the target. This means a unit can only shoot at targets entirely in front of its front edge.
Then make an attack roll using the shooter’s Shoot value.

Extra Pivot
The unit makes one additional pivot, following the Pivoting rules.
For example, a unit with Mobility 2 can make up to three pivots during an activation if it chooses the Extra Pivot action. This is the only way Mobility 0 units can pivot.
Strike
Make an attack roll against one enemy the unit is touching, using its Melee value. 
Rally
Remove 1 strain from the unit.
Attack rolls
Resolve shots and strikes as follows:
Build the pool. Take as many dice as the attacker’s Melee (for a Strike) or Shoot (for a Shoot).
Apply modifiers. Add one die for each modifier in the attacker’s favour, and remove one for each modifier against it. Always roll at least one die.
Roll. Each die that scores the target number or more gives the target 1 strain. The target number is the target’s Defence, unless Armour-piercing or Evasion changes it.
Critical hits. For each 6 rolled, roll one extra die against the same target number. A 6 on an extra die doesn’t give another extra die.
Combat modifiers
Outflanking
Unit A is outflanked if an enemy unit touches A’s flank and that enemy isn’t touching any other unit from A’s army. 
The target is outflanked: +1
The attacker is outflanked: -1
The flanking unit doesn’t have to be the attacker or the target. Each modifier applies only once, however many units are flanking. In practice, outflanking only affects Strikes, because units in contact with the enemy can’t shoot or be shot. 
Height
A unit in elevated terrain is higher than a unit that isn’t.
The attacker is higher than the target: +1 die.
The attacker is lower than the target: −1 die.
For the free Strike after a charge, use where the attacker was before it moved.
Roll to attack
Roll the final attack pool. Each result equal to or greater than the target's Defence gives the target one strain.
Armour-piercing
If the attacker’s stat has an asterisk, the target number is at most 3.
Evasion
When shooting at a unit with Mobility 3 or more, the target number is 6. This overrides armour-piercing.
Strain and routing
Mark each strain on the unit with a strain marker. When a unit has 7 or more strain, it routs: remove it from the battlefield along with all its markers.

Clean up
Remove all command markers from units. 
Terrain
A unit is in a terrain feature if any part of its card overlaps the feature’s printed image. The rest of each tile is open ground and has no effect. 
Terrain traits
Impassable
Units can’t overlap impassable terrain, or move or pivot through it. 
Concealing
Concealing terrain blocks line of sight (see Shooting).

Slowing
If a unit is in slowing terrain at any point during a Move, including at the start, that Move can be no longer than 4 inches. So a unit that has already moved more than 4 inches can’t enter slowing terrain during that Move. Units with Defence 4 or less ignore this rule.

Elevated
Blocks line of sight between units that are both outside it. A shooter in elevated terrain can see over other units, but not over terrain. Units in elevated terrain are higher than units outside it (see Height).
