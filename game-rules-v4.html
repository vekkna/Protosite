Seize the Day — V4

Rules as enforced by this app, from the V4 rules document (copied 2026-11-21, updated 2026-11-25). The outlined circular terrain tile is the terrain feature. Boxed notes say how the app reads points the document leaves open.

Overview

Seize the Day is a game of battles for two players. Each player commands an army, moving units across a tabletop battlefield and using dice to resolve shooting and close combat.

You do not need to collect miniatures or prepare an army beforehand. The units are represented by cards, which act as both playing pieces and reference cards. At the start of the game, you take turns choosing these units and placing terrain, creating your armies and battlefield together.

During the game, you move and turn your units to outmanoeuvre the enemy. Good positioning and timing matters: you can gain an advantage by attacking from the flank, holding useful terrain, or staying outside an enemy’s reach.

The game is played over a series of rounds. At the start of each round, both players secretly bid for control of the turn order. A lower bid gives you more control over who acts when, but a higher bid lets you command more units. You must decide which matters more each round.

You win when your opponent is down to 3 units, or half their starting units if that is fewer. The rules are deliberately simple and abstract, focusing on the key decisions of a tabletop battle rather than detailed simulation.

Components

  • 24 unit cards
  • 12 terrain tiles
  • 24 strain markers
  • 12 command markers
  • 1 measuring stick
  • 10 six-sided dice
  • 2 overview cards

Unit basics

Fronts and flanks

A unit’s front is its front edge, including both front corners. Everything else (both side edges, both rear corners and the rear edge) is its flank.

Figure 1. A unit's front and flank: the front edge and two front corners are the front; both side edges, rear corners and rear edge are the flank.

Stats

  • Pace: how far the unit can move forwards, in inches.
  • Mobility: how many pivots the unit can make when moving.
  • Melee: how many dice the unit starts with when it strikes.
  • Shoot: how many dice the unit starts with when it shoots. A unit with no Shoot value can’t shoot.
  • Range: how far the unit can shoot, in inches.
  • Defence: the score each attack die needs to give the unit strain.

There can be asterisks after certain stats; these are explained in Attack rolls, below.

An asterisk after Pace has no effect in V4.

Units are given strain markers when they are hit. Units with 7 are routed and removed from play.

Setup

Prepare the battlefield

Play on an area 4 feet wide and 3 feet deep, with the players sitting opposite each other at the long edges.

Each player’s deployment zone is the strip along their edge, 6 inches deep. This leaves 24 inches between the zones. On a different-sized table, make both zones the same depth and keep the gap between them as close to 24 inches as you can.

First game

For your first game, you can use the prepared armies and battlefield below instead of drafting. Choose a random player to count as having picked first in the draft. This decides who wins a tied bid in round 1. Otherwise, continue with Prepare the Draft Pool.

Prepare the draft pool

  1. Randomly decide which player uses the dark side of the unit cards. The other player uses the light side. The two sides differ only in appearance.
  2. Choose 12 unit cards at random. Lay them out in an evenly spaced grid between the deployment zones, 4 cards across and 3 deep. No card may touch a deployment zone.
  3. Shuffle the terrain tiles so that it’s random which side of each tile is up, then stack them. Players may look at the top tile but not the ones beneath it.
  4. Randomly choose who picks first.

If you have miniatures, you can choose the 12 units to suit your collection instead of drawing them at random, and place the miniatures on the cards.

Draft units and place terrain

Players take turns to pick, starting with the first player, until all 12 units are deployed. On your pick:

  1. Choose a unit from the draft pool. Leave it where it is for now.
  2. Take the top terrain tile without flipping it over.
  3. Place or discard the tile. If it shows only open ground, or you can’t place it legally, return it to the box. Otherwise, place it so that it:
    • overlaps the unit you chose, and
    • doesn’t touch another terrain tile, a deployment zone or the battlefield’s edge.
  4. You may rotate it however you like. If it covers other undrafted units, put them back on top of the tile in the same positions.
  5. Deploy the unit. Place the unit you chose entirely inside your deployment zone, facing any direction, without overlapping another unit.

Winning the game

A side loses immediately when it is down to 3 units, or to half its starting units, whichever is lower. For example, with 6 units each you win when the third enemy unit routs; with 8 units each, when the fifth routs.

Round sequence

Play rounds until someone wins. Each round has three steps:

  1. Bid for Master Tactician
  2. Command units
  3. Clean up

Bid for Master Tactician

The Master Tactician controls the timing of the round, but usually commands fewer units.

  1. Each player secretly chooses a bid, from 1 up to the number of units they have on the battlefield. Set a die to your bid and keep it covered with your hand.
  2. Reveal both bids at the same time.
  3. Each player takes as many command markers as they bid.
  4. The player with the lower bid is the Master Tactician for this round. If the bids are equal, last round’s Master Tactician keeps the title. In round 1, the player who picked second in the draft wins a tie.

Example: Brian secretly sets a die to 4, Irina secretly sets one to 2. They reveal them at the same time. Brian gets 4 command markers, Irina 2. Irina becomes the Master Tactician.

Command units

The command phase has up to three turns:

  1. The Master Tactician’s first turn. The Master Tactician activates units one at a time. After each activation, they choose whether to activate another unit or pass. They may pass without activating any units.
  2. The other player’s turn. The other player activates units in the same way until they pass. This is their only turn this round.
  3. The Master Tactician’s second turn. The Master Tactician may spend any command markers they have left in the same way, then passes.

A player with no command markers left must pass. Any command markers not spent by the end of the phase are lost.

Measuring, stating intentions and resolving disputes

You may measure distances and check lines of sight at any time. When you move a unit, state your intended result so that disagreements can be resolved before the position becomes difficult to reconstruct. For example:

  • I will move my archers here, where they’ll have a clear shot at your spearmen and remain outside your cavalry's charge range.
  • I will pivot my swords to protect their flank from your cavalry.
  • I will leave my fanatics 6 inches from your Heavy Infantry, so I can charge next round but you can’t charge me.

If you can’t resolve a dispute, roll a die to determine who’s right.

You can use your overview cards to work out hypothetical moves before making them.

Activating units

  1. Choose one of your units that doesn’t have a command marker on it.
  2. Place one of your command markers on it.
  3. The unit may take one action: Manoeuvre, Shoot or Strike.

Because the marker stays on the unit until clean-up, each unit can be activated only once per round.

Actions

Manoeuvre

A unit touching an enemy’s front can’t Manoeuvre, unless it belongs to the Master Tactician and is moving backwards.

The enemy’s front edge includes its front corners. A unit touching only enemies’ flanks or rears may Manoeuvre as usual, but can’t move into an enemy it touches. The Master Tactician’s unit may pivot first if it has room, but its Manoeuvre must then be a move backwards, even if the pivot took it out of contact.

Choose a direction, then move the unit either forwards up to its Pace or backwards up to half its Pace. It can’t switch between forwards and backwards during the Manoeuvre.

Units can’t move over units, impassable terrain, or the battlefield’s edge.

Pivoting

A unit pivots by rotating by up to 90 degrees through its centre.

During its Manoeuvre, the unit may pivot as many times as its Mobility. Each pivot may be at the start, end or during the Manoeuvre. If during the Manoeuvre, it carries on afterwards in the same direction chosen above (forwards or backwards) relative to its new facing.

If the unit doesn’t move forward or backwards, it may pivot one additional time (this lets Drill 0 units pivot if they don’t move).

Once a unit has used that additional pivot it can’t move for the rest of its activation.

A unit may not make contact with another unit or blocking terrain when pivoting.

The app reads blocking terrain as impassable terrain, and also stops a pivot at the battlefield’s edge. A unit already touching something may turn away from it, but not into it. Flush against another unit’s edge it can’t turn either way, since a corner would push into it; touching only at or near a corner, it can usually turn one way.

Charging

A unit may only make contact with an enemy while moving forwards. When it does:

  1. its Manoeuvre ends immediately (you do not line it up with its target), and
  2. it may Strike as a free extra action.

At the very start of the charging unit’s Manoeuvre, draw a line from the centre of its front edge to the centre of the target. If this line passes through the target’s front edge, you are only allowed to make contact with its front edge. Otherwise, you may make contact with any part you can reach.

The target’s front corners count as part of its front edge. When you select a unit, a dashed line runs from its front to each enemy it could reach; a gold front edge means this unit may only charge that enemy’s front this activation. Pivots and moves made during the Manoeuvre don’t change this.

Shoot

Choose one of your unengaged missile units.

  • Unengaged: it isn’t touching an enemy.
  • Missile unit: it has a Shoot value.

Choose a target that’s unengaged, in range and visible.

  • Visible: a straight line can be drawn from the centre of the shooter’s front edge to the centre of any of the target’s edges without first hitting concealing terrain or a unit (including the shooter and the target).
  • Range: that line is no longer than the shooter’s Range.

Then make an attack roll using the shooter’s Shoot value.

The shooter’s own card blocks lines too, so a line can’t go backwards through it. The target doesn’t need to be wholly in front of the shooter.

Strike

A unit is shaken while an enemy’s front edge is touching its flank and that enemy is not touching another of your units. Shaken units can’t strike.

Example: if unit A is shaken as B is flanking it, and then C charges B, A is no longer shaken.

Make an attack roll against one enemy the unit is touching, using its Melee value.

The enemy may be touching the striker’s front or flank.

Attack rolls

Roll dice equal to the attacker’s Shoot value (when shooting) or Melee value (when striking). Each result that equals or beats the target’s Defence deals one strain. There are no other dice modifiers, and 6s roll no bonus dice.

  • Armour-piercing: if the Shoot or Melee value you are using has an asterisk, you need 3 or the target’s Defence, whichever is lower.
  • Loose formation: when shooting at a unit with an asterisk after its Defence, you always need 6. This overrides Armour-piercing.

Add a strain marker to the target for each strain dealt. If it now has 7 or more strain, it routs: remove the unit and all its markers from the battlefield.

Clean up and end of round

Remove all command markers from units, then start the next round’s bidding.

Terrain

A unit is in a terrain feature if any part of its card overlaps the feature’s printed image. The rest of each tile is open ground and has no effect.

Terrain traits

Impassable

Units can’t move over impassable terrain.

Concealing

Concealing terrain blocks line of sight.

Obscuring

More than 2 inches of obscuring terrain blocks line of sight.

None of the terrain in this set is obscuring, so this has no effect in the app.

Slowing

If a unit is in slowing terrain at any point during its activation, including at the start, it can’t move more than 4 inches. So a unit that has already moved more than 4 inches can’t enter slowing terrain during its activation.

Elevated

Concealing between units that are both outside it. A shooter in elevated terrain can see over other units, but not over concealing terrain. Units in elevated terrain are higher than units outside it (see Height).

The document has no Height section yet. In the app, being higher gives no dice.

Sample terrain

  • Hill: Elevated.
  • Forest: Concealing; Slowing.
  • Mountain: Concealing; Impassable.
  • Swamp: Impassable.
  • Field: Slowing.
